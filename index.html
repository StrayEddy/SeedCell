Design + simulation · uncertified research · the food sibling of HiveCell

No one should go hungry because they cannot afford food.

SeedCell is a public, self-cleaning machine that turns long-life dry ingredients into a fresh, warm, nutritious flatbread on demand. Not a vending machine — a food syringe: it briefly opens, delivers food, then cleans itself.

How it works Safety first Read the source

A guaranteed daily ration, not a restaurant

SeedCell guarantees basic nutrition: warm, cheap, portable, eaten by hand, no packaging, almost no residue. Think of it as a daily survival ration. It is designed around the same priorities as HiveCell — self-cleaning, vandal resistance, reliability, low maintenance, long lifespan, public accessibility — with food safety on top.

Fresh, not stored

Dry ingredients are baked to order — no perishable stock, no packaging, no fridge to clean.

One moving part

A single heated piston in a fixed bore does the forming, cooking, delivery, and cleaning.

Minimal contact

The whole food path is one chamber, one transfer, one delivery surface. Everything else stays dry.

The hot syringe

The engineering goal is not making food — it is minimizing food-contact surfaces. A single heated piston moves inside a fixed heated bore and does every job in turn.

  1. User approaches; a privacy-preserving ration check (no fingerprint, no database).
  2. The piston retracts, opening a short cook chamber in the bore.
  3. Dry blend is metered in; water jets hydrate and mix it — no blade.
  4. The piston presses the dough; two hot faces conduction-bake the flatbread.
  5. The cook kill-step is proven — or the batch is diverted to waste.
  6. The piston ejects the flatbread through the mouth die; the user takes it.
  7. The piston parks deep and self-cleans: scrape · steam · heat-sterilize · dry.
  8. The face returns flush — the machine is a plain wall again.
Why a syringe? Fewest moving parts → best reliability, cost, and simplicity. A round bore has no internal corners, so a single circular lip wipes the entire food-contact surface every stroke. The piston face is the flush wall, the cook platen, the delivery surface, and the cleaning interface — all at once.

Fortified protein flatbread

Whole-wheat, chickpea and lentil flour, a little oil, salt, water, and a micronutrient premix — a balanced ration, cheap and complete. First-order model (scripts/dosing.py):

The food is designed to release cleanly: a low-adhesion surface, a sacrificial dry-flour boundary layer that bakes into the crust and leaves with the bread, and a lean formula that shrinks and self-releases. The best way to clean a surface is to never let food stick to it (ADR-0008).

Safety is the whole problem

SeedCell makes food for vulnerable people, unattended, for years. The core invariant is non-negotiable: a batch that is not provably cooked and served on a provably clean surface is diverted to waste — never to a person. Enforced by a headless self-test on every push.

Clean for the next person

Because the food is designed to leave and the contact area is tiny, most sanitation is mechanical + thermal — and nearly free.

Scrape

The piston's own lips wipe the whole bore perimeter as it retracts — the syringe-native squeegee.

Steam + heat

A steam ring sanitizes; the surfaces are already ~230 °C from the bake, so dry-heat sterilization is essentially free.

No chemicals (if it holds)

Thermal + steam should suffice for cooked-starch residue — pending the bench measurement that decides it.

Biggest open risk. Food residue + release is the master variable — it decides whether the machine can be chemical-free and self-cleaning at all, yet is only estimated across a ~1000× range. It must be measured on a real baked coupon (docs/residue_bench_test.md) — the highest-value next experiment and the best first contribution.

Built in the open — how to help

SeedCell is open-source hardware research (FreeCAD · Godot · Blender). The most valuable contributions right now are physical de-risking, not more CAD.

Measure the residue

~$150–250 of kitchen gear to measure SeedCell's master variable on a baked coupon (ADR-0011).

Prove the clean

The hardest open problem: a sensor that proves a food surface is clean, every cycle, unattended (SF2).

Nail the lethality

Set the real cook F-value target for a low-moisture legume flatbread (SF1).

Uncertified research — do not build this to feed anyone. Design + first-order simulation only; no built hardware, no microbiological validation, no food-safety certification.
